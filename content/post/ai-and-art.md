That's a heavy headline, but I do have some thoughts on this. I don't have answers, mind you, but I do have some things that came to mind this weekend while I was working on a project.

## AI Art Issues

The main issue that I've been noddling on is the use of AI to create art. It's something I've been bothered by since people were using it to draw really crappy diagrams and people with six fingers were showing up on posters everywhere. We're largley beyond that issue and AI has started whipping up images that look pretty good. They all seem to be drawn by the same "artist" so to speak, so we can recognize it easily, but the words now look like words and people's faces aren't mushed together. The use of AI art has accelerated for normal people too. It's not just nerds putting this stuff together. Grandman needs a flyer for her mahjong tournament and she's making that happen without much issue.

I've admittedly used it plenty to scratch pad ideas or ask it what it would draw for something. I've often taken that drawing and mixed it up on my own in GIMP to make something useful for me and something with a little more human touch, but it's becoming less and less of a requirement for me to mess around with it. That's where my ethical challenges come in. 

The question I came upon yesterday was whether I should actually be bothered with this at all.

## What is Art?

I am a software developer by trade. I write code for applications so they do what a user wants. In my personal life, that user is often me, but I'm also a professional so there are people paying me to create this code. I'm not as protective over it as some might be, but I'm often proud of the work I do to make things make sense, keep things organized, and eventually find their way into a finished project. 

You might say that code only works one way, but every developer will have their fingerprints on it. They might comment their code a certain way. They might use docstrings more liberally that some folks. They might choose to lift all the complicated stuff out of a main function into some other function, or they might love some long-winded if/else statements. If this isn't making sense to you, just consider that everyone writes things differently and it often shows.

That brings me to the question of, "What is art?" Some people might view their code as a beautiful piece of art because they spent a lot of time and care on it. People may consider it math, but to the coder, it's something they put their heart into. Maybe that is what art is? It's a creation of something that means something to you and something you can put your mark on that sets it apart somehow uniquely from others. Admitedly, there is always some kind boilerplate code where things are done one way because it's proven to be reliable and performant, but there is plenty of other stuff that matters to the coder and can be more unique in nature.

## Skills Gaps

Which brings me to my final point and the thing I've been trying to make sense of in my own head. 

> Is it as valid a use case for an artist to use AI coding to finish a project, as it is for a coder to use AI to make images to finish a project?

It came to mind because I cannot draw. I love to doodle. I think drawing is an incredible skill. I can't do it. I make little drawings that make me happy but it's a limitation that has held me back for a long time. I often can't pay for artist help, so I simply never complete the idea that's in my head. I just think about it, accept that it'll never be done, and go on my way. 

In the age of AI, I now have the ability to take my creative vision all the way to the end. I can build out the parts I know how to build out. I can brainstorm, I can dream, I can architecht. I can put effort into the parts I have the talent and skills for. Then, when I need images for the project, I can work with an AI to fill that skills gap. In my most recent project, I doodled what I could and then I handed that doodle to AI and told it to make it look actually professional. It did a decent job! It didn't ace it because it still looks very much like AI, but it was my artwork FINISHED. Something I never could have done by myself. It actually helped me finish something I've dreamed about for years.

I've heard of artists using AI to code a website so that they could get their art in places that they couldn't have done before. They can now use AI to put together a scrolling art page, or a gallery where it was way more difficult before. They didn't have to pay a web designer or squarespace. They can just throw together a page, ask AI how to deploy it, and get step by step instructions. 

There is still work in both of these cases. There is still effort being put forth to make the thing we're thinking of, we're just approaching it from different places. Artists using AI to build a webpage is only slightly different than coders using AI to get images. From both sides of the situation, you could say that work is being stolen, or the human element is being removed.

I'm not trying to defend AI, but I think the playing field is more level that it might seem. We're not putting this toothpaste back in the tube. We're not going back and it's up to us to figure out how to still provide value, or more importantly satisfy our souls in this new era. We're all made to make things, and we can keep doing that.

I do have a genuine concern that the use of AI is going to keep us from reaching out to other humans for help as often as we might. I think there is a big problem in us isolating ourselves further. That is a larger issue that has to do with the internet, society, and AI.

At the end of the day, I think using AI to close a skills gap that you were never going to pay for is a better option than letting your idea sit undeveleped and unexplored because you lack a certain ability. You have wonderful expression that can now be set free properly thanks to having some technology by your side to make it happen. There was a time when Photoshop art was new and it allowed people who couldn't use a paintbrush to make digital art that still allowed people to feel something. I know that's not an apples to apples argument, but I think there's a nugget of wisdom in there if we'll accept it.

All I'm saying is that you need to go out there and make your thing. The tools are there. Don't let anything get in your way.
