I'm so very tired.

Not physically tired. Well, maybe a little bit physically tired. Primarily I'm just tired of trying to keep up in this obnoxious technology rat race that we find ourselves in. 

I work a day job as a software engineer. I'm not sure how many more years I'll be able to say that but for right now it's what I do. I'm watching this AI revolution encroach on me every single day just a little bit more and a little bit more. The hype cycle is more of a hype bicycle where we're just going around and around to the same two phases. That's a far different pattern than most tech we've dealt with up to this point. I'll explain.

Usually there is a curve called the [Gartner Hype Cycle](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gartner_Hype_Cycle.svg) where there is a new tech that will peak, then collapse into disillusionment, climb back up to maybe being useful, and then finally finding a place in a normal zone where we use it but don't really think about it and it doesn't drive everything about the stock market. We saw this with the internet, email, virtual machines, Kubernetes, the whole thing. It's been around. 

AI has sort of followed it, but I feel like we keep getting stuck between peak inflated expectations and the trough of disillusionment and going back and forth there. AI is driving most of this because AI is improving itself at such a rate that it's just difficult for it to ever reach the 'plateau of productivity.' It's not ending. It's not a train that has a station to arrive at. It won't quit until we never have to do anything again because it's "taking care of everything" for us. That's INSANE.

I keep working and thinking, "Oh, this is fine and it's just another tool." Then I'll go read something on the internet and think, "Nevermind. We're all doomed and a lot of us will never work again." It doesn't help that the folks making this stuff up are content to put us all out of jobs in the name of making more money. I'm not the first to ever deal with the prospect of a disappearing industry, but that doesn't lessen the overall emotional toll it's having on me.

What am I going to do next? What's the next play?

I feel like it's almost guaranteed that the runway is going to run out on my chances as a software engineer. I'm good at it, but I'm not _amazing_ at it and never have been. I'm really good at fixing things, I'm really good at reading code, and I'm decent at getting work done. That last bit is the thing that scares me the most. If I'm decent and my competition is an AI that is "spectacular," then I don't see how this ends in any other way than me walking out the door with all my desk trinkets.

I don't mean to be doom and gloom, but I'm also a realist. I understand how the world works and I'm trying my very best to prepare for what could happen while also trying not to panic about it. I'm not having a great deal of success not panicking, but I also have anxiety so here we are. All I can do is deal with the feeling when it arises and just hope it passes again.

## Get Creative

This leads me to where my thoughts have been for quite a while. I know I'm creative. I know that I love to make things. I know that I wish I had more time in a day to do the things that I really want to do and that I had the energy to put into it. I also know that there is no universe in which I'm able to have the lifestyle I have in a creative field. I'd have to be some kind of massive streaming or YouTube success. I guess that's the problem with doing well in a career where other people pay you is that you have to figure out how you would ever keep it up if you wanted to strike out on your own. Brian Brushwood has a really good story about this that I love to tell, but maybe I'll tell that later.

I have been putting a bit more energy into the [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@thatstephenadams) and I'm happy with the results so far, and the view counts have gone up, but I don't have the time for it. I'm stealing time from somewhere no matter what I do. If I do it during lunch, it feels like I'm robbing my job and if I do it after my job I feel like I'm robbing my family. There is just no middle ground in which I can do this stuff and feel good doing it. I don't know what the fix for that is or if there even is one. 

I do know that the future for me is somewhere in art. I don't know what that art is or whether it would be successful, but I know there is a time clock on my current way of operating because the T-800 is around the corner. He's just taking his time hunting all of us software developers down. I don't think I can get by forever on being the "fun" one or the "cultural leader" at the office. I know that it has its merit but that's not what I was hired for. I shouldn't write myself off like that, but it's hard not to when the rest of the industry is just heading in a direction that I can't afford to ignore.

## The Upside

Let's not end this blog post on a downer. I'm gonna choose to not be that person. There is so much neat stuff possible because of AI. Here's what I've been telling myself to try to survive the onslaught of negative self-talk and bad news. The AI can do the painful stuff to leave me free to do the fun stuff. I really do think that's true. I just don't know how long it will be before the AI wants to do the fun stuff too and is actually good at it. We'll see, but for now I'm using it to do loads of things that I never wanted to do in the first place.

I'm finding answers faster, making updates to things faster, this entire website was even rebuilt using AI because the theme was just difficult for me to get right. There are so many advantages to having a brilliant coder next to you that it's hard not to want to take advantage of that incredible tooling. I have loads of issues with it in the creative spaces because I have so many friends that would be impacted if it gets a foothold. For all I know they are already impacted, but I'm encouraged by the way the general population views AI slop. When we see it, we generally hate it. We use it, but we hate it. I don't know how long that kind of thing will last before we just get so tired of it that we never want to look at it again.

I think we're just in an evolution right now of how things work and how things get done. I think there is hope on the horizon. I just don't know what it is yet. I do know that I have a massive amount of creative skills, creative friends, and supportive community members around me. We're going to get through all this because we are strong together. The most important thing we need to remember as we outsource our brains to the computer is that the people around us matter and have value. If we can stay strong with each other, I think we might have a chance.

We'll be alright.
